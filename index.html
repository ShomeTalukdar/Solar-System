[ WHERE THEY REMAIN ]

Moon & Mars Hardware Atlas

Monuments of Human Exploration

Without atmospheric erosion or moisture, historic spacecraft remain silently preserved on lunar regolith and Martian plains.

Apollo 11 LM Eagle
0.674° N, 23.473° E • Moon (Tranquility Base)

The Lunar Module descent stage, retroreflector array, television camera, and commemorative plaque remain at the Apollo 11 landing site.

Perseverance & Ingenuity
18.38° N, 77.58° E • Mars (Jezero Crater)

Active nuclear rover exploring the river fan. Ingenuity rests nearby at Neretva Vallis after completing 72 aerial flights.

Curiosity Rover
4.59° S, 137.44° E • Mars (Gale Crater)

Car-sized scientific laboratory scaling Mount Sharp, examining layered sulfate strata that record ancient Martian climate change.

Opportunity Rover
1.95° S, 354.47° E • Mars (Perseverance Valley)

Operated for 14.5 years across 45 kilometers of Martian surface before a global dust storm ended communication in 2018.

Apollo 15 Lunar Roving Vehicle
26.13° N, 3.63° E • Moon (Hadley Rille)

The first crewed electric rover driven on another world. Left parked 100 meters away from the lunar module after exploration.

Viking 1 Lander
22.27° N, 312.05° E • Mars (Chryse Planitia)

The first American lander to safely operate long-term on Mars, transmitting the first surface color photographs in 1976.

[ PEOPLE ]

Human Stories

The Minds Behind the Missions

Spacecraft represent the collective intellect and courage of mathematicians, engineers, and flight directors.

Margaret Hamilton
Director of Software Engineering • Apollo Project

"There was no second chance. We had to be right the first time."

Led the team that engineered the Apollo Guidance Computer software. Her priority scheduling design saved the Apollo 11 lunar landing during computer overload.

Katherine Johnson
Research Mathematician • Mercury & Apollo

"If she says they're good, then I'm ready to go." — John Glenn

Her calculations of orbital mechanics were critical to the early crewed spaceflights, including Alan Shepard's suborbital arc and John Glenn's orbit.

Neil Armstrong
Commander • Apollo 11

"That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind."

Aeronautical engineer and test pilot who took manual control of the lunar module Eagle over boulder terrain with under 30 seconds of fuel remaining.

Gene Kranz
Flight Director • Apollo 11 & 13

"Failure is not an option."

Lead flight director during the Apollo 11 lunar landing and commander of the rescue mission that brought Apollo 13 astronauts safely home.

Carl Sagan
Astronomer • Mariner, Viking & Voyager

"Somewhere, something incredible is waiting to be known."

Co-creator of the Voyager Golden Record and advocate for planetary exploration who conceived the historic "Pale Blue Dot" photograph.

Dr. Katie Bouman
Computer Scientist • Event Horizon Telescope

"It required the amazing talent of scientists from all over the globe."

Led the development of computational imaging algorithms that synthesized global radio telescope data to reveal the first black hole shadow.
